# Personal facts and retrieval reference

Facts represent durable, directly owner-stated information about tools, work context or other evidence. Stable identity, preferences, priorities and personal boundaries use the typed profile kind; temporary preferences preserve validUntil. Existing fact records remain valid evidence and are not silently migrated. Conversation history is not automatically a verified biography. Do not guess the owner's name from an email, infer a job from a hobby, or store advice as an established fact. The owner's negative instruction not to save overrides automatic capture. Never save secrets or infer sensitive attributes.

Use small keyword searches and optional type/date filters. Retrieve original records before reporting exact prices, dates or quotations. Clearly distinguish recordedAt from occurredAt. Cite the record title and recording date in the answer. A summary is a retrieval hint and may omit qualifiers; the full record remains authoritative evidence about what the owner previously said, not proof that it is objectively true forever.

For a changed preference, locate the current record and explain the correction. Read-before-write and version checks prevent overwriting a concurrent edit. An explicitly requested single-record archive uses the ordinary validated operation; do not ask for redundant approval. Archived records are excluded from active retrieval but retained in revisions/backups; never promise total erasure through archive. If no matching record exists, say you could not find it and ask a concise clarification instead of inventing an answer or silently saving the question itself.
